## TL;DR

AI coaching does not help every salesperson equally. The reported randomized field experiments show the largest gains among middle performers, weaker outcomes for bottom performers facing information overload, and resistance among top performers. Reducing feedback for bottom performers and combining AI with human managers improves results.

## Key Claims

- AI coaching effects follow an inverted-U pattern across baseline salesperson performance.
- Middle performers benefit most from algorithmic coaching.
- Bottom performers can be overwhelmed by too much feedback.
- Top performers may discount or resist advice from an AI coach.
- Restricting feedback load can improve outcomes for bottom performers.
- An AI-human coaching assemblage can outperform AI-only or manager-only coaching.

## Important Details

- The study reports randomized field experiments rather than only observational correlations.
- The useful unit of design is not “give everyone more feedback”; it is matching feedback amount, framing, and escalation to the recipient.
- Human managers add context, trust, judgment, and motivational framing that an automated coach may lack.
- The public abstract supports the directional claims above, but effect sizes and full methodological details were not available in the captured access.

## My Notes

- This source materially upgrades the sales-coaching page from practitioner guidance to experimental evidence.
- The product implication is segmented coaching: novice/bottom performers need narrow, prioritized interventions; middle performers can absorb more systematic feedback; top performers may need collaborative, evidence-rich framing.
- Human escalation is not merely a safety fallback; it can be part of the higher-performing coaching design.
- Confidence should remain bounded because the full paper and effect sizes were not available during this ingest.

## Open Questions

- How large and durable were the effects across the experiments?
- Which sales contexts, incentive structures, and cultures moderate the inverted-U pattern?
- Do modern language-model coaches reproduce the same overload and aversion effects?
