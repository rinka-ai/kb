# HCI Research

## Overview

This collection preserves original HCI research with inspectable methods and evidence, especially work on human-AI interaction, mixed initiative, uncertainty, user control, accessibility, and evaluation.

## Sources

- [[2019-05-02-guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction]] — CHI 2019 synthesis and iterative validation of 18 observable guidelines for capability expectations, contextual assistance, failure recovery, explanation, learning, feedback, and global control.
- [[2024-06-22-homogenization-effects-of-large-language-models-on-human-creative-ideation]] — C&C 2024 within-subjects study separating ChatGPT's gains in idea fluency and category coverage from its cross-user homogenization and lower felt ownership.
- [[2022-04-29-ai-chains-transparent-and-controllable-human-ai-interaction]] — CHI 2022 within-subjects study of editable LLM chains, intermediate artifacts, local versus global steering, output quality, transparency, control, debugging, and the trade-off between structured execution and free exploration.
- [[2024-05-11-promptcharm-multimodal-prompting-and-refinement]] — CHI 2024 mixed-initiative text-to-image study of prompt/style exploration, attention-based steering, local inpainting, version recovery, novice performance, and the limits of bundled short-task evaluation.
- [[2026-08-04-people-ai-guidebook]] — Google PAIR's living practitioner guide to user-value selection, autonomy, interaction policy, mental models, feedback and control, graceful failure, calibrated trust, data/model evolution, and lifecycle evaluation.
- [[2024-06-29-promptinfuser-ai-ui-design-workflows]] — DIS 2024 within-subjects study of 14 professional designers using live LLM prompts inside Figma mockups, showing benefits and frictions of coupling model behavior to UI design through medium-fidelity prototypes.
- [[2023-04-19-why-johnny-can-t-prompt-how-non-ai-experts-try-and-fail-to-design-llm-prompts]] — CHI 2023 qualitative design-probe study of ten non-expert prompt designers, exposing one-example overgeneralization, weak regression testing, instruction-scope confusion, and misleading human-social analogies.
