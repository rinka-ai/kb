# Follow the Lead: An FTC Staff Perspective on Lead Generation

- Path: raw/articles/official-docs/2016-09-01-ftc-follow-the-lead.md

## TL;DR

The FTC's staff perspective maps how consumer information moves through lead-generation markets and warns that opaque collection, resale, misleading interfaces, and poorly controlled downstream use can create deception and privacy harms. Old CRM records are therefore not automatically permission to restart marketing.

## Key Claims

- Lead generation connects consumers with sellers but can obscure who collects data and how it will be used.
- Consumers may not understand that submitted information can be sold or distributed to multiple parties.
- Deceptive claims and insufficient disclosure can create consumer-protection risk.
- Sensitive data and broad downstream distribution increase privacy and security risk.
- Buyers and sellers of leads need governance over provenance, representations, and downstream practices.

## Important Details

- The report arose from the FTC's “Follow the Lead” workshop and reflects staff perspective rather than a new statute.
- The analysis covers publishers, lead generators, aggregators, and end buyers.
- Risk depends on the collection context, disclosure, data sensitivity, accuracy, consent, and downstream use.
- For database reactivation, a record's existence is not enough; teams should retain consent provenance, acquisition context, suppression state, and jurisdiction.
- This source predates newer AI outreach tools but remains directly relevant to the data supply chain they operate on.
